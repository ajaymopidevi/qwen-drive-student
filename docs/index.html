Qwen-Drive Student

Distilling a driving VLM's 3D perception into a small camera-only model

Ajay Narasimha Mopidevi

Code ↗ Teacher report ↗ nuScenes ↗ Interactive viewer ↓

TL;DR

Qwen-Drive-1.0 gets 3D detection, BEV map segmentation and 3D occupancy from six cameras, on top of a 4-billion-parameter vision-language model that takes about 4 seconds a frame on this GPU. A 24M-parameter student replaces the VLM, and the perception head was rebuilt block by block under pre-set accuracy gates. The final model runs in 105 ms and keeps 92% of the teacher's detection mAP on the full nuScenes validation set.

Next steps

The student so far replaces the teacher's VLM for the BEV perception head only. Qwen-Drive-1.0 also answers driving questions with the VLM's language decoder and plans ego trajectories with a separate Planning Expert. The next two milestones move those two onto the small model, using the same approach: distill the teacher, then gate each step.

1 · Driving VQA

The teacher has no separate VQA head: its unchanged Qwen3.5-4B language decoder answers driving and general questions as text. The student has no language model, so this milestone adds one. Plan: attach a small language decoder to the student's image and BEV features and distill it from the teacher's answers on driving VQA, scored against the teacher on the same questions.

2 · Planning head

The teacher's Planning Expert is a separate flow-matching transformer of about 1.1B parameters, trained by imitation and then reinforcement learning, that outputs 50 ego waypoints over 5 s. It does not read the VLM's final features but its cached attention keys and values from eight layers, which the student does not produce. Two routes: train the student to produce stand-ins for those caches, or condition a smaller planner on the student's BEV outputs. Either way, one small model would produce boxes, map, occupancy and a trajectory; the milestone measures the planning gap to the teacher and the latency added to the 105 ms frame.
